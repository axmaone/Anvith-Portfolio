## The brief

Explain how semiconductors are manufactured to people with no engineering background, accurately, and make them care about why precision matters.

## Learning objectives

1. Describe the semiconductor manufacturing process.
2. Explain the six major manufacturing stages.
3. Identify key quality and precision requirements.
4. Recognise common manufacturing challenges.

## The design approach

- **Simple to complex.** Start with *what is a semiconductor* and *why it matters* (phones, cars, medical devices, AI), then walk the process.
- **Six learner-sized stages.** Dozens of fab steps grouped into six: silicon preparation, wafer manufacturing, circuit patterning, layer formation, interconnection and inspection, packaging and testing.
- **A walkthrough you can follow muted.** The video above is the module itself, edited for the portfolio: a stage tracker, a key idea per screen, and the scenario played out with a real click on the best option.

## The scenario

You are a **production supervisor**. Sensors detect a rise in airborne particles in the cleanroom. Production is on schedule, and stopping will delay deliveries.

- **Continue production?** Particles damage circuit patterns; batches need rework.
- **Pause and investigate?** A faulty air filter is found; affected wafers are isolated quickly.
- **Rely on final testing?** Defects are found late, and resources are wasted.

Learners see the consequences of each choice, then return to try the others.

## Media justification

- **Process animation** shows steps no one can watch with the naked eye.
- **Infographics and icons** simplify dense technical information.
- **A process-flow visual** holds the sequence together.
- **The scenario** turns knowledge into a decision, which is what sticks.
- **An audio summary** is offered as an alternative recap.
