## The brief

Virtualisation is invisible: nobody can *see* a hypervisor. The summary had to make an abstract layer concrete and show why a 1960s idea now runs the cloud, in about a minute and a half.

## Learning objectives

1. Define virtualisation and its purpose.
2. Identify the major milestones in its evolution.
3. Describe how virtualisation supports modern IT.
4. Compare its role across different eras.

## How the summary video teaches

- **Start on a screen the learner knows.** A desktop with a cursor asks the question: how can one computer behave like many?
- **Build the idea from blocks.** Hardware, then the hypervisor, then the virtual machines stack up like toy bricks, each one placed as it is named.
- **Let each era look like its era.** The 1960s play as a silent film with intertitles, the 1980s on a green CRT terminal, the late 1990s in a retro desktop window, the 2000s as a server room shrinking from ten machines to two, and the 2010s as a cloud keynote. The style change itself tells learners that time has moved on.
- **End with a check, not a recap.** One quick question ("what does a hypervisor actually do?") asks learners to retrieve the idea before the closing line lands.

## Behind it

Scripted, voiced and built as code in HyperFrames, with licensed music and sound effects, and captions you can switch on in the player. It condenses my storyboarded e-learning module, which goes on to cover the forms of virtualisation (network, server, storage, desktop and application) and why they matter: cost, utilisation, disaster recovery and the foundation of cloud computing.
