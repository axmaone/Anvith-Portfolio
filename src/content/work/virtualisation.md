## The brief

Virtualisation is invisible: nobody can *see* a hypervisor. The module had to make an abstract layer concrete and show why a 1960s idea now runs the cloud.

## Learning objectives

1. Define virtualisation and its purpose.
2. Identify the major milestones in its evolution.
3. Describe how virtualisation supports modern IT.
4. Compare its role across different eras.

## The design approach

- **Ground it in the learner's laptop.** "You could run Windows and Linux side by side without needing two laptops." The abstract idea enters through something familiar.
- **One diagram that grows.** Hardware, then the hypervisor layer, then the virtual machines appear one at a time, each when it is named.
- **History as problem → solution → impact.** Each era (1960s origins, the 1970s–80s decline, the late-1990s revival, 2000s consolidation, the 2010s cloud) is framed the same way, so learners compare eras rather than memorise dates.
- **A timeline that never leaves the screen.** In the walkthrough above, an era tracker and a key idea per decade keep the thread clear.

## Beyond the history

The module closes with the forms of virtualisation (network, server, storage, desktop and application) and why they matter: cost, utilisation, disaster recovery and the foundation of cloud computing. A one-screen summary and an optional audio recap wrap it up.
