## The problem

Loan-to-value is a ratio, three tiers and a regulator's cap. On a slide it reads as a table people forget. Learners need to *see* why a necklace can raise a certain amount and no more.

## The approach

I built the whole explainer around one character and one object: **Priya and her 92-gram necklace**. Every number on screen is a step in her story, in the order a branch officer would work it out:

1. **Weigh**: net weight of 22-karat gold.
2. **Price**: value at an illustrative rate.
3. **Cap**: the RBI LTV tier for that loan size.
4. **Borrow**: what she actually needs, and the headroom left.
5. **Watch**: what happens if the gold price falls.

The isometric "table-top" world keeps everything on one stage, so the eye never has to re-orient. Bars grow physically out of the gold, which makes the ratio tangible. Captions carry every line so it works muted.

## Why it works for learning

- **One worked example beats three definitions.** The rule is learned through a case, then summarised in a legend at the end.
- **Sequenced like the job.** The five steps match the real valuation flow, so the video doubles as a job aid.
- **Low cognitive load.** One new idea per beat, with signalling (callouts, colour) pointing at exactly one thing at a time.
